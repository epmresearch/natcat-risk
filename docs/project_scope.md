## 1. Working thesis title
**Probabilistic Landslide Risk Assessment of the Sea-to-Sky Highway: Integrating Direct Infrastructure Damage, Indirect Economic Losses, and Resilience Planning with CLIMADA**

## 2. Context and problem
Mountain transportation corridors exposed to landslides and rockfalls can incur two distinct forms of loss: (a) direct physical damage and restoration costs, and (b) indirect losses generated by road closures, detours, delayed or canceled trips, and disruption to local businesses or tourism. The provisional case study is the Highway 99 Vancouver–Whistler corridor in British Columbia, Canada.

The case-study location and use of CLIMADA are **working specifications supplied for the thesis project**. They are not explicitly named in the uploaded administrative research letters and must be confirmed in the research protocol.

## 3. Main objective
Build a transparent and reproducible spatial and probabilistic risk-analysis workflow that combines landslide hazard scenarios, road asset vulnerability, road-network disruption, and economic impact models to estimate direct and indirect losses and test the cost-effectiveness of mitigation measures.

## 4. Research questions
- **RQ1:** Which highway segments are most exposed to landslide-related hazards under clearly defined study-area and hazard-type assumptions?
- **RQ2:** What direct infrastructure losses can be expected under plausible event and vulnerability scenarios?
- **RQ3:** How do the location and duration of highway closures alter travel connectivity and impose indirect economic costs (travel disruption, local economic activity and tourism)?
- **RQ4:** Given adequately validated annual event frequencies, what are annual expected direct, indirect and total losses, and how sensitive are they to uncertainty?
- **RQ5:** Which mitigation and resilience interventions offer the greatest risk reduction per dollar invested?

## 5. Conceptual system boundary
`Landslide trigger / susceptibility / occurrence model -> road-impact scenarios -> CLIMADA Hazard + Exposure + Impact Function -> direct damage -> closure probability and duration -> transport + economic disruption model -> indirect losses -> total event losses -> AAL (if frequencies defensible) -> mitigation scenario analysis / CBA`

- Physical hazard reaching road **is not identical** to closure.
- Binary occurrence rasters should **not** be treated as calibrated intensity measures without justification.
- CLIMADA Petals' Landslide hazard tools can accept historic occurrences or probability rasters, but do not independently solve landslide runout mechanics.
- For indirect loss, a separate road network and economics module is expected; the CLIMADA impact engine alone does not provide this whole process.

## 6. Planned key variables and output metrics
| Variable | Planned interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Event or annual frequency | Empirically defensible annual occurrence frequency / uncertainty |
| Hazard footprint / severity | Geospatial event affecting road segments |
| Direct loss (CAD) | Physical repair, replacement and cleanup; define accounting convention |
| Closure duration (hours/days) | Partial or full closure with recovery model |
| Travel disruption | Number of affected trips and additional travel cost/time |
| Indirect loss (CAD) | Net additional economic costs and lost value added, avoiding double counting |
| AAL (CAD/year) | Expected annual direct, indirect, and total loss (only when probabilistic model is defensible) |
| Mitigation | Change in hazard impact, closure probability or recovery duration |
| CBA | Net present value, benefit-cost ratio, annual avoided loss |

## 7. Candidate inputs (not yet acquired or verified)
1. Terrain: DEM / LiDAR, slope, aspect, relevant geological units.
2. Hazard: event inventory with date/type/location; rainfall and triggering factors; appropriate occurrence model.
3. Road infrastructure: highway centerline/segments, bridge and retaining-wall assets, unit replacement costs.
4. Transport: highway traffic counts, seasonal traffic, alternate road network, traveler time values.
5. Economic: Whistler tourism/activity indicators, freight and commuting patterns, closure-event evidence.
6. Mitigation: technical alternatives, implementation/maintenance cost, effect on hazard and downtime.

## 8. Validation / uncertainty commitments
- Report hazard inventory completeness, positional uncertainty and under-reporting.
- Validate hotspot/event model on independent spatial or temporal data where feasible.
- Sensitivity-test impact functions, physical replacement costs, closure duration, detour options and economic assumptions.
- Report best-estimate and uncertainty ranges; label purely illustrative scenarios as illustrative.
- AAL requires event frequency; otherwise use scenario-based losses and explicitly avoid claiming probabilistic AAL.
